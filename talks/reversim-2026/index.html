The game in 15 seconds

One word. One number. Your cards, not theirs, and never the black one.

Quick reminder of the rules. The spymaster knows which cards belong to which team. They say one word and a number, and their team guesses. If you touch the other team's card, you've helped them. If you touch the black card, you lose on the spot.

2026: we bolt an LLM
onto everything.

So how far do you get with classic NLP and some geometry?

It's 2026, and every problem gets an LLM bolted on. I wanted to try the opposite. How far can classic NLP get me, with some honest geometry and no billions of parameters?
The one idea

Words are points.

This is the whole trick. A model like fastText turns every word into a point in space. Words that appear in similar contexts end up close together. Train, ship and helicopter sit in one corner. Dog and cat sit over there.
So a clue is…

the point closest to my cards,
farthest from theirs,
and very far from the assassin.

Score every word in a clean vocabulary. Keep the best.

So a clue is just a point. I want it close to my cards, far from the other team's cards, and very, very far from the assassin. That gives me a scoring function. I score every word in the vocabulary and keep the best one.

Then I tested it against a real Hebrew LLM, DictaLM, at 12 billion parameters. Blending fastText with the ConceptNet knowledge graph made clues twenty percent better and kept them safe. The LLM kept breaking the rules and making up words.

Scale isn't what
this task needs.

It's a semantic-similarity problem disguised as a board game.

I'm not claiming the small model won. My point is that scale isn't the bottleneck here. Codenames is a semantic-similarity problem disguised as a board game, and similarity is exactly what vectors are built for.

Give it the most frequent words and you get "of", "also", word fragments. It's garbage. Narrow it to medium-frequency nouns and adjectives and clean out the slurs, and the clues got dramatically better overnight.
Surprise #2

Sometimes the honest answer is
"there is no safe clue."

The second surprise: the best feature is the copilot saying no. When every candidate drifts toward the assassin, it tells you there's no safe clue. That saves more games than any clever pun.
Why Hebrew is harder

Rule: a clue can't share
a root with a card.

פחד · מפחיד · פחדן

כלב · כלבלב

Now for Hebrew. The rule says a clue can't share a root with any card on the board. In English you'd just compare strings. In Hebrew, fear, scary and coward all share one root, and the word for puppy has the whole word for dog inside it.
So it's layered
  • Root lexicon from Wiktionary
  • Containment check for reduplication & diminutives
  • Similarity gate so false roots don't block good clues

Deterministic. Offline. No LLM.

None of the Hebrew NLP tools I tried gave me a reliable root. So I built the check in layers: a root lexicon taken from Wiktionary, a containment check for words like puppy, and a similarity gate on top. It's all deterministic and offline.
